# Research basis: five Great Lakes Hexagenia atlas (October 2026)

The atlas is **not a ranked list of live 2026 swarm sites**. The source dates are exposed to readers. Historical nymph studies cannot establish today's swarms or modern population counts. Map points are broad, approximate public town references — **never original sampling GPS coordinates**.

## Source-backed geography

- **Erie, Bass Islands / western basin:** USGS long-term 1997–2005 benthic surveys documented widespread Hexagenia nymphs in the western basin, varying year by year, including island areas. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/distribution-and-abundance-burrowing-mayflies-hexagenia-spp-lake-erie-1997-2005
- **Erie, western shores and Port Clinton orientation:** USGS documented large historical adult shore swarms and recovery across the western basin. Port Clinton is an orientation point rather than a confirmed local emergence sample. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/recovery-burrowing-mayflies-ephemeroptera-ephemeridae-hexagenia-western-lake-erie
- **Huron, Tawas / Saginaw Bay:** 2010 adult swarms were counted at Tawas-area illuminated sites. Nymph densities surveyed 2009–2012 remained much lower than historic bay levels. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0380133014000562
- **Michigan, northern Green Bay:** USGS 2001 nymphs at only six of 48 Green Bay samples, with highest density/biomass at north end. Do not call Door County a proven modern mass-hatch hotspot. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/burrowing-mayflies-indicators-ecosystem-health-status-populations-western-lake-erie
- **Superior, Chequamegon Bay:** USGS 2002 research documented Hexagenia nymphs in suitable cohesive sediments; does not establish recent adult mass swarms. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/burrowing-mayflies-indicators-ecosystem-health-status-populations-two-western-lake
- **Superior, St. Louis estuary:** Same USGS study found patchy populations and absence at 11 sampled locations affected by sediment contamination. Do not call all Duluth Harbor suitable habitat.
- **Ontario, Bay of Quinte:** Canadian State of the Great Lakes 2003 report confirms burrowing mayflies had returned; not a current lake-specific live count or mass-hatch proof. https://publications.gc.ca/collections/Collection/En40-11-35-2003E.pdf
- **Bonus connecting water, Lake St. Clair:** Michigan Sea Grant describes seasonal Hexagenia emergence in the western reaches. It must remain labeled as a connecting lake, not one of the five. https://www.michiganseagrant.org/blog/2024/03/11/when-will-the-mayflies-arrive/

## Product integrity gates

1. Only existing NDBC stations 45165 (Western Erie) and 45163 (Saginaw Bay) receive live NOAA/NWS status; temperature measures local surface water, not lakebed or population abundance.
2. Existing iNaturalist research-grade evidence and curated hatch-report endpoints continue to cover their original two regions, never automatically cloned to other lake sites.
3. The often-quoted 68°F cue is a contextual near-sediment condition for shallow western Lake Erie, not a universal five-lake hatch threshold. Water depth, oxygen, substrate and nymph populations matter.
4. No date-specific swarm forecasts, site rankings or hatch probabilities are generated from old studies.
5. The map shows approximate public orientation points and a separately styled NOAA instrument layer, not specific access sites or sampling coordinates. OpenStreetMap attribution must remain visible and site cards must work without map tiles.
6. Future live coverage of Chequamegon Bay, Green Bay, Bay of Quinte etc. requires independently validated sensor identifiers, freshness, measured depth and published source semantics before onboarding.
