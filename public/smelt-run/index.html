03 / Read the water, not just the calendar

What actually starts a run?

A living spring calendar: conditions change, and the uncertainty should change with them.

SIGNAL 01

Ice-out and warming water

Rainbow smelt gather around lake tributaries after ice-out. Spawning can begin as streams approach the low-to-mid 40s°F and continues across a broader spring range. Only direct USGS water measurements receive a measured-temperature label.

SIGNAL 02

Gentle spring rain

Warming rain is a classic trigger, but high, fast streams can be unsafe. A forecast chance of rain is not evidence that a stream has risen or fish have arrived.

SIGNAL 03

Darkness and fish reports

Spawning moves are often concentrated at night. Watch conditions, then confirm locally before traveling. This tracker never upgrades a region to “run confirmed” without an independent observed-fish source.

The decision rule

Promising conditions mean it could be worth scouting — not that you'll catch smelt. Regions lacking local stream thermometers stay on weather watch, even during a traditional peak week. This matters because Great Lakes smelt abundance is far below its historic highs.

04 / Go prepared

Michigan smelt-dipping rules

Check the actual stream reach, public access, and current DNR digest before dipping; designated trout streams and listed exceptions matter.

March 1 – May 31 · hand nets

Great Lakes and specified connecting waters/tributary reaches, plus selected inland waters. Rules vary by waterbody.

March 20 – May 31 · dip nets

Generally streams except designated trout streams and specifically closed reaches. Dip nets must not exceed 9×9 feet.

Two gallons daily possession

The Michigan DNR smelt possession limit is two gallons. Additional possession and transport restrictions can apply.

Access and safety first

No netting within 100 feet of dams; respect private property, fast spring currents, slippery bottoms, and cold water. Carry light, appropriate gear, and a PFD.

05 / Common questions

Smelt dipping FAQ

When is the Michigan smelt run?

Usually in spring, especially April into May along Lake Superior tributaries. Dates vary with water temperatures and ice-out. The tool avoids exact run dates without fish observations.

Which Michigan smelt-dipping spots are historically best?

The DNR's older location inventory lists the Tahquamenon River, Roxbury Creek, Galloway Creek, Anna River, and Falls River among good opportunities. That does not establish the status of fish in any given year.

What water temperature triggers smelt spawning?

Michigan Sea Grant describes initial movement as water reaches around 42–44°F following warming and rain. Smelt may also spawn in warmer water; look for trends, not one magic number.

Does a warm air forecast mean smelt are running?

No. Weather is not stream temperature, and stream temperature is not a fish count. The tracker labels each independently.

Can I dip smelt anywhere on a listed river?

No. DNR rules exclude designated trout streams, certain reaches, and dam proximity. Private property requires permission. Check the detailed regulatory map and signs.
