How to read the signals

The model checks seasonal timing first. A fresh USGS measurement is required before calling conditions favorable. Water temperature is influential but does not determine emergence by itself. Weather refines the activity window, especially for drifting duns and spinner falls.

Trout safety first: if the measured water reaches 68°F or higher, the tool displays a heat caution rather than encouraging catch-and-release trout fishing.

This first release does not claim hatch sighting verification, species-specific calibrated probabilities, or an exact hour of emergence.

Why branches matter

The North Branch, South Branch, and mainstem can experience different thermal conditions and hatch timing. Downstream dam-influenced stations are not a substitute for Grayling or the Holy Waters. The historic Grayling USGS gauge is no longer a source of live water temperature.

For the Holy Waters, we deliberately do not publish a made-up live temperature from a downstream site. Exact-reach monitoring can be added when a verified measurement source is available.

Great Lakes expansion

This is the Au Sable watershed launch of a broader Great Lakes mayfly system. Other Michigan trout rivers are natural next locations. Nearshore Hexagenia emergences on lakes and bays require a separate habitat-and-surface-water model rather than reusing trout-stream rules.
