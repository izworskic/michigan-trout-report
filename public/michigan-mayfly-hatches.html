Help me narrow down the fly

Look first at size, body color and when it appeared. The results suggest photos to compare—not a definitive species ID.

Select clues to see suggested species photos.

First make sure it's a mayfly: adult mayflies normally hold large front wings upright at rest and have long tail filaments. Caddisflies commonly fold hairy wings roof-like; midges lack long mayfly tails. A mayfly dun has more opaque wings; a mature spinner usually has clearer wings. Take a sharp side-view photo and include a coin or fingertip for scale.

Important: A gauge recording favorable water temperatures is not a confirmed hatch. At 68°F or warmer, the trout-fishing signal is suppressed for catch-and-release safety. Missing water temperatures are never estimated from air temperature or a different river.

Don't confuse the water

Au Sable Holy Water: outstanding for classic spring hatches. Michigan DNR's natural river plan notes that Brown Drake and Hex hatches favor sand-and-silt sections above and below the flies-only reach.

Pere Marquette: the Scottville gauge is downstream of the renowned flies-only water near Baldwin. Its flow cannot prove a Baldwin Hex hatch.

Manistee: upper-river and Sherman gauges are separate sections; neither represents the entire river.

Muskegon: Croton Dam tailwater conditions can differ from the warmer downstream habitat.

Boardman: a Brown Bridge Road flow gauge is not a measured Traverse City hatch.

Signature mayfly seasons

Broad field-guide windows, not fixed hatch dates
Hendrickson · Ephemerella subvariaMid-April – May

Afternoon mayflies on warming spring water. Au Sable, Manistee, and Pere Marquette dry-fly fishing highlights.

Brown Drake · Ephemera simulansLate May – June

Large dusk mayflies; sand and soft bottom matter more than a famous access location.

Hexagenia · giant Michigan mayflyMid-June – July

Michigan's legendary nighttime dry-fly event. Au Sable, Manistee, Pere Marquette and other suitable slow, silty trout water.

Sulphur · Ephemerella groupsLate May – July

Often evening activity, sometimes mixed with other mayflies. A temperature match never guarantees a rising fish.
