The 68°F emergence watch

Surface proxy, not lakebed

Michigan Sea Grant describes a shallow western Lake Erie pattern: several days after lake-surface water reaches about 20°C (68°F), sediments may warm enough for Hexagenia to emerge. That timing is not proven for every Great Lakes site.

What should I watch?

1. Surface warming

Look for a fresh local buoy temperature approaching or exceeding 68°F. In shallow western Erie, sediments can warm a few days later.

2. Evening winds

Light winds may make flight activity more noticeable near the water. Direction can shift where swarms arrive onshore. A good-looking evening isn't an observed swarm.

3. Documented specimens

Research Grade photo observations show where Hexagenia were identified in a broad surrounding region. These records do not establish lake emergence, a mass swarm or a particular shoreline location. Follow the links to review the original evidence.

A lake hatch isn't the Au Sable Hex hatch

Different habitat

Lake Hexagenia nymphs burrow in sediment. Water-column oxygen and bottom habitat influence their populations. A fishable trout-river riffle doesn't behave like a Great Lakes bay.

Different 68°F meaning

Here, 68°F is a reported lake emergence-development reference. On the trout-river tool, 68°F is a caution for catch-and-release fishing. We do not mix the two rules.

Different confidence

A fresh NOAA buoy is evidence of water temperature, not evidence that Hexagenia are present. Historic Saginaw Bay and Lake Erie population studies don't establish current local abundance.

Frequently asked questions

When do the Great Lakes mayflies hatch?

Hexagenia swarms commonly occur across June through August in shallow Great Lakes waters. In western Lake Erie the first big periods can begin in early summer, depending on bottom warming and nymph populations. There is no single statewide hatch date.

Does 68°F mean the mayflies are hatching tonight?

No. Michigan Sea Grant identifies about 20°C (68°F) at the sediment as a relevant biological threshold. A surface buoy reading is a proxy and may lead bottom temperature by days. We do not claim emergence has begun without observations.

Why does Saginaw Bay sometimes say temperature unavailable?

The Saginaw Bay buoy is seasonal and may not publish live water temperatures in autumn or winter. Missing and stale measurements are displayed honestly instead of substituting Toledo, a river gauge, or Lake Huron open-water averages.

Will the tracker tell me which shoreline gets the swarms?

Not yet. Wind direction and speed influence where airborne mayflies move, but a buoy and NWS point forecast cannot verify where insects emerged or where they will accumulate.

What's the difference between lake flies and Hex on the Au Sable?

Both can include large mayflies in the genus Hexagenia, but Great Lakes emergence depends on lake-bottom habitat and temperatures; the Au Sable tool focuses on trout-stream conditions and fly-fishing windows. Open the Au Sable mayfly predictor.
