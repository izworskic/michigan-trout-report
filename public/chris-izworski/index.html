Chris Izworski

Founder and builder of Michigan Trout Report, writing from Bay City, Michigan on the Saginaw Bay watershed.

Chris Izworski built Michigan Trout Report to answer the question that matters before a trout trip: what is the river actually doing right now? The site brings together live USGS streamflow and water-temperature observations, National Weather Service weather context, historical comparisons, maps, and river-specific information so Michigan anglers do not have to assemble the same picture from a stack of separate government pages.

The project grew out of fishing Michigan rivers and small trout streams, especially in the northern Lower Peninsula. The goal is practical rather than promotional: use real source data, show where it came from, distinguish observations from interpretation, and say when the available data is incomplete.

How Chris approaches the data

Michigan Trout Report is built around source fidelity. Gauge readings come from USGS. Weather context comes from the National Weather Service. When a measurement is missing or stale, the site should show that limitation rather than silently inventing a value. That same source-first approach carries through the river maps, hatch information, access notes, and daily reporting.

Background

Chris Izworski spent more than a decade leading county 911 operations in Michigan before moving into public-safety technology. That operating background shaped how he builds public-data tools: make the source visible, keep the decision path understandable, and design for people who need a useful answer rather than another dashboard to decipher. His broader professional background, published work, media coverage, and project history are documented on his verified profile at chrisizworski.com.

Outside public safety, Chris gardens on Saginaw Bay, paddles and camps, fishes Michigan trout water, and builds Michigan and Great Lakes information projects around the things he uses himself.
